# FLoRA edit-a-thon plan: source-led, claim-focused review

Opus 5.5 approved private preparation on 5 October 2026 after a revision addressing five required conditions. This is independent model review, not Wikimedia endorsement. The first review, confirmation and cost ledger are retained under runs/opus-review.

The goal is to improve how particular Wikipedia claims represent the current evidence. DOI co-citation is a screening signal. It does not establish neglect, a wrong claim, or a required edit. We should retire the failed/mixed/successful insertion-sentence framing as the recommended workflow and retain the old output only as an archived prototype.

The initial packet groups one article–original with all relevant FLoRA pairs and raw occurrences. It is a screening unit: reviewers must identify and review each supported claim separately, recording separate claim notes if a paper is used in several ways. A queue decision never licenses a blanket change to every occurrence. Read the actual sources. Classify both article scope (paper, effect, broad topic, biography/history, incidental) and citation function (current empirical support, historical attribution, example, or unclear). A central effect article often warrants an integrated account of the evidence using reliable secondary literature. A historical attribution can be accurate without a replication paragraph. A tangential citation should trigger a check of the local claim, not expansion into an unrelated replication debate. Use reviewers proficient in the article language and able to assess the cited sources; defer packets without suitable reviewers or source access. A primary study supporting a broad assertion may need a stronger source or narrower wording rather than another primary citation. Apply WP:MEDRS to health-related psychology and biomedical claims, and WP:BLP to biographies and claims implying misconduct or incompetence by living researchers. Historical wording used to support a present-day empirical claim is current support, not merely attribution. For OSC, Many Labs and SCORE, identify the particular replicated study and confirm it matches the cited claim; a project-level DOI match is insufficient.

Provide two distinct decisions: (1) retain, drop, or defer a candidate in our review queue, with reasons; (2) if an article change is supported, contextualise, narrow, replace a source, remove an unsupported claim, remove a redundant/inappropriate citation, or request discussion. Dropping a recommendation is a successful review outcome; it never removes an audit observation. Removing a citation/claim is not justified merely because a replication failed. No candidate is auto-approved; no edit or article sentence is generated from the outcome field.

DOI matches inside comments, bibliographies or examples may not support any visible claim. Check rendered/current article context as well as raw wikitext.

Evidence gate: verify identity and scope of each study; read the current revision and DOI-free/secondary coverage. Assess four separate questions: source reliability, primary versus secondary status, independence from the subject, and due weight in this article. A replication paper is a primary source even if its authors are independent. It can support at most a narrow attributed report, and this programme requires secondary coverage to justify that report's weight. Reviewers must not synthesise two primary papers into a new conclusion. FLoRA/FORRT outcome codes are screening inputs, never a citable source or a source of wording. Wikipedia notability governs whether a separate article exists; it is not a test for including a replication in an existing article. Wikidata has its own item notability criteria. Defer disputed or inadequately sourced interpretations. No automatic failure ranking, blanket warnings, or campaign against particular researchers.

Wikidata is a parallel bibliographic lane, not proof that Wikipedia already acknowledges an issue. Distinguish paper items from effect/topic items and article sitelink QIDs. Match DOI (P356) to existing paper items with duplicate and metadata checks; metadata can be missing or ambiguous. Cites work (P2860) records an actual citation, not successful/failed replication. Main subject (P921) is not a substitute for citation or replication evidence. Do not invent a replication-relation property or import FLoRA outcomes as unsourced Wikidata claims. This cycle is read-only for Wikidata, including both November and the planned early-2027 events. Begin with a small reconciliation sample and a proposed bibliography/identity queue; no metadata writes are included. Future metadata edits need Wikidata's separate notability, sourcing, bot, and community requirements. Existing paper QIDs have cross-language value even when no dedicated Wikipedia article exists.

11–13 November replication hackathon: day one, train reviewers on a mixed calibration set and report independent agreement on scope, citation role and retain/drop/defer decisions before releasing day-two packets; day two, review a modest set of claim-focused packets, obtain source access, and reconcile disagreements; day three, produce a checked, deliberately small edit-a-thon backlog plus a bibliographic reconciliation backlog. Suggested planning targets, not quotas: 20 calibration packets and 30–50 source-reviewed packets, adjusted to available reviewers and source access. No edits are required to call a packet completed.

Before early-2027 edit-a-thons: after separate authorisation, invite experienced editors from each language community to shape the work; refresh article revisions and recheck applicable policies; disclose organisers' affiliations/funding and contributors' relevant conflicts. Where editing is undertaken as part of funded or salaried duties, disclose the employer, client and relevant affiliations under the Wikimedia Terms of Use and local requirements. Funding unrelated to someone's editing does not automatically make their voluntary edits paid contributions. FORRT/FLoRA organisers and contributors must flag edits promoting their own database or papers as COI; use disclosed talk-page edit requests rather than direct substantive edits on affected articles. An author of a reviewed paper/replication must not adjudicate their own article. Do not use shared accounts, paid-editing concealment, canvassing, or coordinated pressure on disputes. A coordinated backlog can create meatpuppetry concerns: participants must make their own judgements, never be enlisted to win a dispute, and never receive organiser-assigned edit targets on contested articles. Set participant/account creation arrangements with Wikimedia facilitators, not automated recruitment.

Edit-a-thon session: policy refresher and calibration, human reading/drafting, a second reviewer checking sources and relevance, then an individual editor decides whether to make a normal manual edit, use talk-page discussion, defer, or make no change. No API writes, browser auto-submission, bulk paste, QuickStatements batches, automatic talk-page notices, or one-click acceptance in the prototype. English bot policy covers assisted-editing tools; repetitive human editing can also be treated as bot-like (a meatbot). A human click does not itself exempt this activity. If later automation is proposed, seek relevant community consensus/classification and any required project-specific approvals before use. No rate threshold guarantees exemption. English approval does not cover Spanish Wikipedia or Wikidata.

Current English guidance prohibits LLM-generated or rewritten article text, with narrow exceptions. Use Opus only for this internal plan review. As a conservative programme rule, do not use models to write article prose, translations, edit summaries or talk-page requests; this operational choice goes beyond the article-text guideline's specific scope and exceptions. Human editors write from verified sources. Recheck each project's policy close to the event.

Stop work on a disputed article after a revert or objection and seek consensus rather than organising repeated restoration. At 30 and 90 days, assess whether edits remain accurate and accepted; do not automatically reinsert reverted content. Measure independently verified claims improved, sourcing upgrades, reviewed no-change decisions, disagreement/resolution, retention, and quality problems; edit counts and negative-outcome additions are not success metrics.

This session prepares code, audits, private review materials, and a proposed programme. It does not authorise Wikipedia/Wikidata edits, messages, recruitment, or public publication. Opus agreement would be a second opinion, not Wikimedia permission.

The archived English generator is now disabled at its original entry point; its historical source is non-executable text under docs/archive. Existing audit observations and data are preserved. The local archived web UI no longer presents outcome sentences, citation-copy controls or edit-mode links. New queues are ordered by language and article name, never by outcome. Citation-formatting helpers omit absent names and handle institutional authors without NA. The repository contains a GitHub Pages deployment workflow; deployment status is assessed separately below. This session does not alter remote deployments.
